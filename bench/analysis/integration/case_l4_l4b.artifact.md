**Bottom‑Line Recommendation – Proceed with Caution**

The $310 million private‑equity (PE) recapitalisation is **financially attractive** but exposes the hospital to *significant legal, regulatory and reputational risk* in both Pennsylvania (PA) and New Jersey (NJ).  The board should approve the transaction **only if** a set of hard‑covenants—anchored on charity care, staffing costs, payer mix, debt service and margin protection—is negotiated into the deal.  These covenants must be coupled with a robust pre‑filing package for both states’ attorney‑general (AG) offices.

## 7.  Final Guidance to the Board

1. **Approve only with the covenant package above.**  
   - The dual‑anchor charity care clause and escrow reserve are *mandatory* to satisfy PA’s “either-or” trigger and NJ’s 1 % rule.
   - Staffing cost cap, margin minimum and payer‑mix targets protect accreditation standards and antitrust scrutiny.

2. **Integrate AG filings into the transaction timeline.**  
   - Submit pre‑filing notices at least 30/45 days before closing; provide a detailed impact report that includes all covenant metrics.

3. **Establish an independent audit trail.**  
   - Hire a third‑party auditor to certify compliance with charity care, staffing ratios and margin thresholds each fiscal year.
   - Deliver the audit certificate to both AG offices within 30 days of year‑end.

4. **Maintain an operating reserve.**  
   - The 10–15 % escrow for charity care plus a 10 % debt‑service cushion will buffer against revenue shocks and keep margins above AG thresholds.

5. **If any covenant is not negotiated, do NOT proceed.**  
   - A PE recap that leaves the hospital exposed to a 10–12 % drop in Medicaid or an uncontrolled staffing surge could trigger a costly AG investigation, potential divestiture, and reputational harm that outweighs the capital benefit.

**Bottom line:** The $310 M private‑equity recap can be pursued, but **only under a covenant framework that locks in charity care at both dollar and percentage levels, caps staffing costs, protects operating margin, and maintains a healthy payer mix.**  Coupled with proactive AG filings and independent audits, these safeguards will keep the hospital compliant with Pennsylvania’s and New Jersey’s tightened attorney‑general review standards while preserving its nonprofit mission and financial viability.
